There are two tiny
ridges hiding on your
keyboard, on the F
key and the J
key, and they're not
a manufacturing defect.
Rest your fingers on
the middle row and
your left hand lands
on A S D
F, your right on
J K L semicolon.
Those two bumps sit
right under your index
fingers, so a touch
typist finds that exact
spot without ever looking
down.
Lift your hands off
the keyboard, look away,
set them back down,
and your fingers still
land on F and
J first, guided by
feel alone.
That's why trained typists
watch the screen the
whole time instead of
hunting for keys.
The same trick shows
up again on the
number pad, where the
5 key carries a
matching raised dot, marking
its center.
Nobody can point to
who actually added it,
or exactly when.
One patent people credit
online for this doesn't
even cover F and
J, it covers two
completely different keys.
Manufacturers just kept doing
it anyway, because it
worked, keyboard after keyboard,
for decades.
Two tiny bumps, one
blind reflex, still guiding
your hands right now.
