That little tab on
the end of your
foil box looks like
nothing.
It's not nothing.
Push it in, and
it locks the whole
roll in place inside
the box.
Reynolds didn't add these
end locks until 1996,
and even then, the
instructions are printed in
letters so small almost
nobody notices them: press
here to lock roll.
Here's the problem they're
solving.
Without the tabs pushed
in, that cardboard tube
slides and spins every
time you pull on
the foil, so the
sheet tears jagged and
crooked.
Push both tabs in,
on either end, and
the tube stops moving
completely.
The foil stays flat
against the box's built-in
cutting edge, so one
clean pull gives you
one clean, straight sheet.
It's not only foil.
Plastic wrap and parchment
paper boxes hide the
exact same tabs, doing
the exact same job,
and almost nobody uses
them.
So the next time
a torn edge comes
out ragged, the box
isn't broken.
