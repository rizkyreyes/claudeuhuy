Facts (publisher pages not opened; locked from concordant search excerpts, 20 Oct 2026):
- The dent is called a punt. Wine Spectator (Dr. Vinny, https://www.winespectator.com/articles/why-is-there-an-indentation-in-the-bottom-of-a-wine-bottle-46387): it dates to hand-blown glass, glassblowers pushed the seam of the base up so the bottle stands level and doesn't end in a sharp point of glass; it may have added strength; machine-made bottles are stronger so today it is mostly tradition except in sparkling wine, where it spreads out internal pressure; a deep punt says nothing about quality and can be a bit of a gimmick; the sediment-trap idea is unproven.
- Concordant: Gear Patrol (https://www.gearpatrol.com/drinks/bottom-of-wine-bottle-indent-dimple/): punt is a hand-glassblowing leftover, no real relation between punt depth and quality. Culinary Lore (https://culinarylore.com/beverage-science:why-do-wine-bottles-have-a-concave-bottom/): sediment explanation makes little sense. Mental Floss, The Takeout, VinePair carry the same account.
