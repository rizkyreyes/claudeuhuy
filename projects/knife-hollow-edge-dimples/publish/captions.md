Title: Why Do Some Knives Have Dimples on the Blade? #shorts

Description:
Those dimples are called a hollow edge. Makers say the little pockets let air slip between the blade and the food, so slices let go instead of sticking. It works best on wet, starchy food like potatoes, and cutting away steel also makes the blade a bit thinner and lighter. They aren't stick-proof, and America's Test Kitchen says they matter less on a santoku, which is already thin.
