There's a hole in
the top of your
pen cap.
That's not for the
ink.
The ink already has
its own vent, further
down on the barrel,
letting air in as
you write.
The hole in the
cap does something completely
different.
Most people never notice
it.
It's there so a
child can still breathe
if that cap gets
stuck in their throat.
Between 1970 and 1984,
nine children died in
the UK after choking
on pen caps that
sealed off their windpipe.
So in 1990, Britain
wrote a new safety
standard, BS 7272, requiring
every cap to let
air pass even while
lodged in place.
BIC began drilling that
hole into every Bic
Cristal cap that year,
then applied the rule
to every pen it
sold worldwide.
The standard later became
international as ISO 11540,
and most manufacturers copied
the design.
No UK child has
died from a swallowed
pen cap since.
That hole isn't a
manufacturing defect, and it
isn't decoration either.
It's a tiny airway,
engineered in only after
real children stopped breathing.
