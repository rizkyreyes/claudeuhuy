Facts (search excerpts, 9 Nov 2026; agency pages not fetched in the cloud network, wording kept soft, no health claims):
1. Ultrasonic repellers: FTC staff sent warning letters to 60+ makers/retailers asking for competent and reliable scientific evidence; earlier FTC complaints alleged rodent reactions are temporary at best (habituation) and that the devices do not control insects (FTC announcement reprinted by Iowa State University Extension: https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/article/2001/8-24-2001/ultrasonic.html; https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102f1po/building-a-better-mousetrap-better-have-proof).
2. Bug zappers: University of Delaware study, six residential zappers over 10 weeks, 31 of 13,789 insects killed were biting flies (incl. mosquitoes); authors called zappers worthless for biting-fly reduction (San Gabriel Valley Mosquito and Vector Control District: https://sgvmosquito.specialdistrict.org/are-bug-zappers-really-effective-against-mosquitoes-the-answer-may-surprise-you; Gurney's: https://www.gurneys.com/pages/ybyg_of-bug-zappers-and-bat-houses). Percentage left off the slide (sources round it differently).
3. Mouse bait: University of Missouri Extension "Use a light slather of peanut butter as a bait on the snap trap. Cheese and fruit are good second choices" (https://extension.missouri.edu/news/prevent-mice-and-rats-from-coming-inside); Utah State University Extension: best bait is peanut butter (https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/ipm/structural-pest-id-guide/house-mouse.php).
4. Daddy longlegs: UC Riverside Entomology (Rick Vetter): the name covers harvestmen, cellar spiders and crane flies; harvestmen are not spiders and have no venom glands; no evidence cellar spider venom is the most potent (https://entomology.ucr.edu/news/2022/01/14/are-daddy-longlegs-really-most-venomous-spiders-world; https://www.livescience.com/are-daddy-longlegs-the-most-venomous-spiders).
5. Mothballs outdoors: Florida Dept. of Agriculture (FDACS): mothballs are EPA-registered pesticides, outdoor use to repel snakes/squirrels/moles violates the label and is illegal (https://ccmedia.fdacs.gov/content/download/33195/file/Mothballs-Brochure.pdf); UF/IFAS EDIS PI289: misuse outdoors "with the mistaken idea that this pesticide will repel snakes" (https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/PI289); Georgia Dept. of Agriculture fact sheet (https://agr.georgia.gov/sites/default/files/documents/pest-control/facts-about-mothballs.pdf).
