c05-tech-myths sources (checked 9 Oct 2026 by web search; support.apple.com is blocked by the proxy, so claims come from search excerpts quoting the pages)
1. Closing apps: Apple support says force quit only when an app is unresponsive; Craig Federighi replied "No and no" to whether quitting apps saves battery; iOS suspends background apps. https://www.tweaktown.com/news/97785/apple-confirms-swiping-apps-closed-doesnt-save-you-battery-life/ ; https://www.phonearena.com/news/Apple-officially-confirms-that-closing-recent-apps-wont-improve-your-iPhones-battery-life_id79203 ; https://daringfireball.net/2017/07/you_should_not_force_quit_apps (the "relaunching can use more power" line is reporting, kept as "can")
2. Incognito: Chrome discards local history/cookies on close; Google's own disclaimer says sites, employer/school and ISP can still see activity. https://chromestory.com/2026/07/incognito-mode-what-it-hides/ (quotes the Google disclaimer)
3. Drain to zero: Apple's lithium-ion page says you can plug in whenever you like, no need to run to 0%. https://www.apple.com/au/batteries/why-lithium-ion (via search excerpt)
4. Overnight charging: Apple, Optimized Battery Charging reduces time spent fully charged, delays charging past 80% when it predicts a long session. https://support.apple.com/108055 (via search excerpt); Consumer Reports describes the overnight behavior. https://www.consumerreports.org/smartphones/how-to-extend-iphone-battery-life
5. Bars: T-Mobile says bars are a non-standard estimate of connection strength, not speed, and don't show how busy the network is. https://www.t-mobile.com/dialed-in/wireless/what-do-cell-phone-bars-mean
