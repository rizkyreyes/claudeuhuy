c03-laundry-myths sources (publisher pages blocked by the proxy; claims locked from search excerpts)
1 Cold water: ENERGY STAR (heating water is about 90% of the energy to wash clothes) and U.S. DOE Energy Saver (wash in cold water with cold-water detergent whenever possible; oily stains are the exception). https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/appliances-and-electronics/laundry
2 More detergent: Consumer Reports, too much detergent leaves residue and buildup and doesn't clean better; suds cushion clothes so they rub less. https://www.consumerreports.org/laundry/
3 Lint: U.S. CPSC estimate about 15,500 dryer fires a year; U.S. Fire Administration: failure to clean is the leading cause (about 34%). https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/dryer-fires/
4 Dry Clean tag: FTC Care Labeling Rule guidance: a label needs only one safe method; "Dryclean Only" warns the garment can't be washed. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/clothes-captioning-complying-care-labeling-rule
5 Overloading: Tom's Guide, clothes need room to move; about 80% drum capacity. https://tomsguide.com/home/home-appliances/how-much-laundry-can-you-fit-into-a-full-load-and-still-get-your-items-clean
