## YouTube description
How It's Built #47
The dent in the bottom of a wine bottle is called a punt, and it doesn't tell you anything about the wine's quality. It started with hand-blown glass: the bottom of a blown bottle came out with a rough seam in the middle, so glassblowers pushed it up into the bottle. That way the bottle stands level on a smooth ring and the sharp bit is tucked away, and it may have made the glass stronger too. Today bottles are machine-made: a gob of molten glass at roughly 2,000°F drops into a mold and compressed air blows it into shape, punt included. For still wine the punt is mostly tradition. Sparkling wine is different: the second fermentation in the sealed bottle builds up to about 90 psi (about three times a car tire), and the curved punt helps spread that pressure more evenly around the base. The idea that the punt is there to catch sediment has never been proven, and a deeper punt mostly means more glass, not a better wine.
