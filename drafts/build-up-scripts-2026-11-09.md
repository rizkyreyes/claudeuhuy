# Build-up format drafts (9 Nov 2026)

Format (from a 3D outlier reel, 892x its median): on-screen text names a small everyday object -> problem -> add one part -> next problem -> ... -> pull-out to the whole system; camera mostly holds the same cross-section; last shot echoes the first object. "If you..." + claim opening (see Hook rule), question CTA, no channel name. Drafts only: every number must be verified with a source in facts.md before VO.

## Hook rule for these 4 (Rizky, 10 Nov 2026, from vidIQ hook research)
The FIRST spoken sentence = an "If you..." scenario at the viewer's own home/yard + the claim, in one breath (the "If you..." hook is the top outlier pattern now; claim hooks give the biggest lift for small accounts, so combine both). Frame 1 = the cross-section already cut, the object in it. "THIS IS A ..." stays only as the on-screen text over the first shot. Do NOT copy competitor title/hook wording ("How a Hole in Your Yard Becomes a Well", "If you dig a hole in your Yard..."): vary it as below.
- water-well: "If you dig deep enough in your yard, water fills the hole. Getting it back up is the hard part: suction can't lift it past 33 feet." (verify 33 ft)
- pipe-under-river: "If you need a pipe on the other side of a river, you don't dig through it. You drill under it, and steer the drill like a car."
- lightning-rod: "If lightning hits your roof, it's going into the ground one way or another. A lightning rod doesn't attract it, it just picks the road."
- pile-driver: "If you hit a stake with a hammer, it sinks. The machines that hold up skyscrapers do the same thing, except the hammer lifts itself."

## 1. water-well (3d-22) — "THIS IS A BUCKET"
Bucket on rope -> hand suction pump (can't lift past ~33 ft, atmospheric limit; verify) -> submersible pump at the bottom pushes instead of pulls -> casing so walls don't collapse + slotted screen keeps sand out -> grout seal at top so surface water can't run down the outside -> pressure tank so the pump doesn't start on every tap -> pull-out. CTA: "Would you drink water that came straight out of the ground?" Check: number of US homes on private wells (EPA ~23 million households).

## 2. pipe-under-river (3d-23) — "THIS IS A GARDEN HOSE"
Trench across the riverbed (wrecks river, blocks boats) -> drill rig on bank pushes thin rod at an angle -> slanted bit + tracking sensors steer the curve under the river -> reamer pulled back to enlarge, maybe several passes -> drilling mud holds walls and carries cuttings out -> prewelded pipe string pulled back in one piece -> boat passes above. CTA: "How many pipes do you think are under the river closest to you?"

## 3. lightning-rod (3d-24) — "THIS IS A METAL ROD"
Claim: a lightning rod doesn't attract lightning, it gives it a safe path. No rod: current goes through wiring/pipes -> rod alone does nothing -> thick down conductor outside with gentle bends (no sharp corners) -> ground rod driven deep (verify NFPA 780 length) -> several air terminals on the ridge tied together -> strike: path glows, house stays dark. CTA: "Does your house have one, or are you just hoping?"

## 4. pile-driver (3d-25) — "THIS IS A HAMMER"
Claim: "This hammer and the machine that holds up skyscrapers work the exact same way." Hammer + stake -> building needs a steel pile, driven very deep (verify typical length/depth) -> crane drop hammer, slow to lift every time -> put the weight (ram) in a steel cylinder, falling ram compresses the air -> diesel sprayed in ignites from compression heat (like a diesel engine) -> blast drives the pile down AND throws the ram back up, cycle repeats (verify blows per minute) -> leader frame keeps blows straight, cushion/helmet protects the pile head -> pull-out to a tower standing on piles. CTA: "Would you stand next to one when it fires?"
Title (own, not the reference's): "How Pile Drivers Work: Why Does the Hammer Fly Back Up? #shorts". Cover: cylinder cutaway at the blast, ram thrown up, "IT LIFTS / ITSELF?!".
