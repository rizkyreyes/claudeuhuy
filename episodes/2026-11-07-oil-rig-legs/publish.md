## Caption
Oil platform legs are built lying on their side on land, then flipped upright at sea. The steel frame, called a jacket, is skidded onto a barge, towed out and slid off the back. Its sealed legs keep it floating until crews flood the bottom, so it swings upright and settles on the seabed. Then steel piles are hammered deep into the seabed through sleeves on the legs, grout locks them in, and a crane ship sets the deck on top. In really deep water many platforms float instead, held down by steel tendons. Would you work out there?
