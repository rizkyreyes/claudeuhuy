Toilet flush (gravity tank toilet): claims -> sources (verified 2026-11-01 from search excerpts; page fetches are blocked in the cloud run, so excerpts were cross-checked across sources; weak or single-source details dropped)
- The flush works as a siphon: water from the tank rushes into the bowl through holes under the rim and a siphon jet near the bottom, the trapway fills, and the water falling to the drain pulls the bowl's contents out through the trapway; it stops when air re-enters the trapway (the gurgle):
  https://assist.kohler.com/en/toilets-and-seats/How-a-Toilet-Flushes ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flush_toilet ; https://www.familyhandyman.com/list/how-a-toilet-works/ ; https://scienceinsights.org/how-do-toilets-flush-siphon-tank-bowl-explained/ ; https://www.physics.hku.hk/~phys0607/lectures/chap02.html
- The trapway is a bent passage built into the base; the bowl's standing water level is set by the height of the trap's weir (the top of the bend); water added above it overflows the weir and goes down the drain; that standing water forms the seal that blocks sewer gas:
  https://www.hometips.com/repair-fix/toilet-water-too-high-low.html ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US5887294 (underflow/overflow weir sets the max bowl level, excess goes down the sewer) ; https://www.mrrooter.ca/about/blog/how-a-toilet-bowl-works/
- Handle -> chain -> flapper lifts off the flush valve, the tank empties into the bowl; the flapper drops and seals when the tank is low:
  https://assist.kohler.com/en/toilets-and-seats/How-a-Toilet-Flushes ; https://www.familyhandyman.com/list/how-a-toilet-works/ ; https://mrrooter.com/about/blog/2017/june/how-it-works-the-toilet-tank
- The float drops with the tank water and opens the fill valve; rising water lifts the float and shuts the valve; a small refill tube sends water into the overflow tube to refill the bowl and restore the trap seal:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flush_toilet ; https://www.hometips.com/replace-toilet-fill-valve.html ; https://www.balkanplumbing.com/what-are-the-parts-of-a-toilet/
- The overflow tube is a failsafe: if the fill valve sticks or the float fails, the extra tank water drains into the bowl instead of over the tank onto the floor:
  https://www.hunker.com/12495059/how-to-fix-an-overflow-in-a-toilet-tank/ ; https://www.hometips.com/repair-fix/toilet-keeps-running.html ; https://mrrooter.com/about/blog/2017/june/how-it-works-the-toilet-tank
- US federal maximum 1.6 gallons per flush for new toilets (Energy Policy Act of 1992); WaterSense label 1.28 gpf is voluntary (not used in the VO):
  https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2017-01/documents/ws-products-support-statement-fv-toilets.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-flush_toilet
- CTA (bucket flush) is only a question; background: pouring a bucket in fast enough starts the siphon: https://home.howstuffworks.com/toilet2.htm ; https://www.thisoldhouse.com/plumbing/how-does-a-siphon-work
Cut / not said: the effective date of the 1.6 gpf rule (sources give 1994 vs 1997), older toilets' flush volumes (3.5 vs 3-7 gal), whether the fill valve opens before or after the flapper closes (depends on the valve design, so the VO only says the float opens it), "2 gallons" bucket figure.
