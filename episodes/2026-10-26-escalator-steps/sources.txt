Episode 3d-03 escalator-steps: claim -> source. Page fetch was blocked in this cloud run (WebFetch to wikipedia.org and
howstuffworks.com failed DNS), so claims were locked from concordant search-result excerpts of these pages.

- A pair of chain loops runs the length of the escalator; the steps are attached to them
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/escalator1.htm ; https://wonderopolis.org/wonder/how-do-escalators-work
- A motor at the top turns the drive gear(s) that pull the chains
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/escalator1.htm ; https://encyclopedia.com/education/culture-magazines/escalator
- Each step has two sets of wheels; the set near the top of the step is connected to the chain and pulled by the drive gear,
  the other set glides along its own track, following behind
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/escalator1.htm
- The tracks are spaced/positioned so each step always remains level; the position of the tracks controls the step's orientation
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/escalator1.htm ; https://www.positioniseverything.net/tech-in-plain-sight-escalators/
- At the top and bottom the tracks level off to horizontal, flattening the stairway; each step has grooves so it fits together
  with the steps in front of and behind it during this flattening
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/escalator1.htm
- Comb plates at the landings have teeth that mesh with the grooves in the steps; a clearance between cleats and comb teeth is kept
  small to limit objects getting caught
  -> https://patents.google.com/patent/US4126218 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US5255771A ; https://www.untappedcities.com/cities-101-the-first-working-escalator-was-installed-in-coney-island-brooklyn/
- Handrail moves in the same direction and at (practically) the same speed as the steps (code allows ~2 % deviation), driven from the drive gear
  -> https://www.mesj.ukim.edu.mk/journals/article/download/21/22 ; https://encyclopedia.com/education/culture-magazines/escalator
- The step chain is a loop: steps return underneath to the bottom (orientation on the return run NOT stated: sources disagree)
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/escalator1.htm ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US5020654
- Jesse Reno's "inclined elevator" ran as a novelty ride at the Old Iron Pier, Coney Island, in 1896 (two weeks); it was a
  conveyor-like belt with cast-iron cleats, not steps
  -> https://hagley.org/node/3634 ; https://www.6sqft.com/the-worlds-first-escalator-was-installed-in-coney-island-120-years-ago/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalator
  (said "one of the first", not "the first": Reno's 1892 patent and earlier unbuilt patents make "first" arguable)
- "Escalator" was a registered trademark (Seeberger, assigned to Otis); in 1950 the US Patent Office cancelled it as generic
  (Haughton Elevator Co. v. Seeberger, 85 USPQ 80), partly citing Otis's own generic use
  -> https://www.agip.com/News/en/articles/23506 ; https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2022/08/18/tools-of-the-trade-how-escalators-helped-retail-move-up ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalator
