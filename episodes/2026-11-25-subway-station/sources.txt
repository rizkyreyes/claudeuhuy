Sources: subway-station (3d-32), checked 19 Nov 2026 (cloud run, web search excerpts; worksinprogress.co and flatironcorp.com not reachable from the sandbox).

"Cars are driving right over this subway station while it's being built" / "lay steel beams across, and cover them with deck panels. Traffic goes right back on."
  -> DFI abstract (Fantaye 2016), Second Avenue Subway station: "temporary street decking over the entire excavation", decking on busy Second Avenue
     https://dfi.dcatalog.com/v/DEI-Nov-Dec-2012/?page=60  (DFI magazine) and https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/9780784479742.033
  -> Tunnels & Tunnelling, 96th St project: "installing roadway decking to maintain existing traffic"  https://www.tunnelsandtunnelling.com/?p=30946
  -> Wikipedia/Wikiwand, 96th Street (IND Second Avenue Line): temporary decking formed the top of the boxes and supported Second Avenue traffic
     https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/96th_Street_(IND_Second_Avenue_Line)
  -> 1984 LA Metro Rail utilities report: pavement removed, deck beams placed, decking panels on top  https://libraryarchives.metro.net:443/DPGTL/scrtd/1984-maintenance-of-utilities-during-metro-rail-construction.pdf
"the hole goes sixty-eight feet down"
  -> ASCE chapter (Grigson, Ho, LeMus), 2nd Ave Subway 96th St station: excavation up to 68 ft deep, within ~20 ft of buildings
     https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/9780784479742.033
  (Other sources give 85 ft for the wider excavation and 49 ft (fan site); the best-sourced 68 ft is used and named on screen as the 96th St station.)
"if its dirt walls caved in, the road would drop in with them" (why the walls come first)
  -> general excavation support logic: walls + temporary supports (tiebacks/struts) keep the sides of the excavation from collapsing (Wikipedia "Slurry wall")
     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry_wall   Shown as an illustrative what-if, no numbers.
"So the walls go in first, before anyone digs"
  -> top-down / perimeter-wall-first sequence: "the installation of the perimeter wall is the first stage"; walls usually slurry walls (USDOT 1976 cut-and-cover report; core.ac.uk review)
     https://libraryarchives.metro.net:443/DPGTL/usdot/1976-cut-and-cover-tunneling-volume-1-construction-methods-design-and-activity-variations-final-report-may.pdf
"A clamshell bucket cuts a narrow trench ... kept full of liquid clay called bentonite slurry. It pushes back on the dirt, so the trench holds open with no wall at all."
  -> Wikipedia "Slurry wall": trench kept full of bentonite-water slurry; it provides outward pressure that balances the inward forces and prevents collapse; dug with clamshell bucket or hydromill from guide walls
     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry_wall
  -> Aarsleff "What is a slurry wall?" (filter cake + fluid pressure prevents collapse)  https://aarsleff.co.uk/?p=72056
"Then a steel cage drops in, and concrete is pumped in from the bottom. It's heavier, so it shoves the slurry up and out."
  -> Wikipedia "Slurry wall": steel cage lowered, concrete placed by tremie pipe from the bottom up; the heavier concrete displaces the slurry, which is pumped out, filtered and reused
     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry_wall
  -> Cardiff University slurry-trench paper (concrete displaces the less-dense slurry)  https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/107551/1/Cleall%20P%20-%20Slurry%20trench.pdf
"Panel by panel, that becomes a solid wall, three and a half feet thick."
  -> 96th St station: "3.5-ft-thick reinforced concrete slurry wall panels" on the east and west sides of the street (ENR / Flatiron project page via search excerpts)
     https://enr.com/articles/11812-best-project-airportstunnel  /  https://www.flatironcorp.com/?p=2862
"Water mains and cables get hung from the beams."
  -> US patent 4008577 (cut and cover subway): utilities suspended by hangers from the decking beams until the subway is formed  https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4008577
  -> 1984 LA Metro Rail report: utility support structures attached to the deck beams; DFI abstract: utility relocation and suspension
"As it deepens, steel struts brace the walls apart, so they can't fold in."
  -> braced-cut excavations: wales transfer lateral load from the walls to the struts, struts are compression members (slideshare braced cuts; USDOT 1976 report "wales and struts vs tiebacks"); DFI abstract: staged excavation under an intricate bracing system
"At the bottom, crews pour the floor, then build the station up to the roof. The struts come out, dirt goes back on top, and the street is rebuilt."
  -> 96th St station built bottom-up (Wikiwand 96th St); USDOT 1976 cut-and-cover report (bottom-up: structure built in the open excavation, backfilled, surface restored)
"That's cut and cover, how New York built most of its first subway in 1904."
  -> ASME landmark #31 IRT / nycsubway.org "Engineering Features of the New York Subway": primarily open excavation, the "cut-and-cover" system; opened 27 Oct 1904
     https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/31-interborough-rapid-transit-system
     https://nycsubway.org/wiki/Chapter_14:_Engineering_Features_of_the_New_York_Subway
  -> ASCE Civil Engineering 2024 "The first New York City subway delivers speed" (Parsons chose shallow cut-and-cover)
     https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2024/05/the-first-new-york-city-subway-delivers-speed
