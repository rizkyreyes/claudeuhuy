## YouTube description
How It's Built #35
How plumbers fix a drain under your house without digging up the floor: cured-in-place pipe (CIPP). The crew opens one cleanout, a camera crawls in to find cracks and roots, and the pipe is cleaned. A soft felt tube soaked in resin is pushed in inside out: air pressure unrolls it down the pipe like a sock, pressing the wet resin against the old walls, and because it is still soft it follows the bends. Hot water, steam or UV light cures it into one seamless pipe with no joints for roots. It is designed to stand on its own even if the old pipe crumbles. The liner also covers the side branches, so a small robot with a camera cuts each opening back out. Liners are designed for a 50-year life; the first one went into a London sewer in 1971 and is still in service.
