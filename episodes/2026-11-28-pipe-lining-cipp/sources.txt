# pipe-lining-cipp (3d-35) - claim -> source. Checked 21 Nov 2026 (cloud run) via search-engine excerpts (wikipedia.org and some trade sites are not fetched directly from the sandbox).

"They fixed this broken pipe without breaking the floor. The new pipe was built inside the old one." (= cured-in-place pipe, a trenchless method)
  -> Wikipedia, Cured-in-place pipe: "a trenchless rehabilitation method used to repair existing pipelines ... a jointless, seamless pipe-within-a-pipe" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cured-in-place_pipe
  -> U.S. EPA, Collection Systems Technology Fact Sheet "Trenchless Sewer Rehabilitation" (EPA 832-F-99-032, 1999): trenchless methods use the existing pipe as a host for a new pipe or liner instead of dig-and-replace; CIPP = flexible fabric tube with thermosetting resin, cured to form a new pipe inside the old one https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090508051532/http:/epa.gov/owm/mtb/rehabl.pdf
"Normally, a cracked drain under a house means jackhammers, a trench through your floor" = the conventional dig-and-replace method the EPA sheet contrasts with trenchless (no cost/time numbers claimed).
"the crew opens one cleanout" / "a camera crawls in to find the cracks and the roots, and the old pipe gets scrubbed clean"
  -> pipe is inspected by CCTV and cleaned before lining (Iowa DOT Section 4147 / city CIPP specs, e.g. Tulsa Div IV 410) https://www.cityoftulsa.org/media/17763/div-iv-410-cured-in-place-pipe-updated-january-2022.pdf ; lateral liners installed from a cleanout/access point (KUB 02548 CIPP Lateral Liner spec) https://www.kub.org/uploads/02548_-_Cured-In-Place_Pipe_Lateral_Liner.pdf
"a soft felt tube, soaked in resin" -> Wikipedia CIPP: resin-impregnated felt tube ("wet-out"); EPA sheet (fabric tube keeps the resin in place)
"They push it in inside out. Air pressure unrolls it down the pipe ... pressing the wet resin hard against the old walls" -> Wikipedia CIPP (inverted or pulled in; water or air inversion); US patents 9453597 / 8066499 (air inversion and steam cure), patent text: fluid pressure keeps the liner inflated against the host pipe walls during cure https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9453597
"Because it's still soft, it follows every bend." -> Wikipedia CIPP: because the liner is still wet it can negotiate corners and bends, conforming to the host pipe
"Then they cure it with hot water, steam, or ultraviolet light." -> Wikipedia CIPP (hot water, steam, or UV light with fiberglass tubes); EPA sheet (heat cures the resin)
"The resin hardens into one seamless pipe, with no joints for roots to sneak into." -> Wikipedia CIPP ("jointless, seamless pipe-within-a-pipe"); roots enter through joints/cracks of the old pipe (general; no number)
"It's designed to stand on its own, even if the old pipe crumbles around it." -> design per ASTM F1216 for the "fully deteriorated" host pipe condition (Iowa DOT 4147; KUB lateral spec: designed for fully deteriorated pipe conditions) https://iowadot.gov/erl/current/GS/content/4147.htm
"the liner also sealed over every side branch. So a little robot with a camera rolls in and cuts each opening back out." -> Trenchless Technology "Keeping up with lateral reinstatement" (liner covers the service connections, reopened with a camera-guided cutter) https://trenchlesstechnology.com/keeping-up-with-lateral-reinstatement/ ; US patent 10955081 (lateral reinstatement cutter with camera)
"These liners are designed to last fifty years." -> KUB lateral liner spec (50-year design life) ; City of Tulsa CIPP spec (50-year design life); Iowa DOT 4147 (50-year long-term modulus). Said as "designed to last", not "will last".
"The very first one went into a London sewer in nineteen seventy-one, and it's still in service today." -> NASTT Hall of Fame, Eric Wood (first installation 1971, egg-shaped Victorian sewer in Hackney, London) https://nastt.org/about/hall-of-fame/2013-eric-wood/ ; Wikipedia Insituform / CIPP ("still in service"); Trenchless Technology "Celebrating CIPP at 50" https://trenchlesstechnology.com/celebrating-cipp-at-50/
Dropped: inventor name and patent year (sources disagree: 1970/71 UK vs 1975 US application), cost and time savings, liner thickness, cure temperatures and times, % of US sewers lined.
