## YouTube description
How It's Built #38
That chunky block on a hair dryer plug is a tiny safety switch (an ALCI, appliance leakage current interrupter). Power goes out on one wire and back on the other, both through a ring-shaped sensing coil. If a dryer falls into water and current leaks out another way, out and back stop matching; a leak of about 6 mA makes the coil signal a tiny circuit, an electromagnet snaps the contacts open in a few milliseconds and the reset button pops up. The test button fakes a leak: press reset, then test; if it doesn't pop, stop using the dryer. Before these blocks, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted about 18 hair dryer electrocutions a year, about half of them small children; since 1991 the protection has to work with the dryer on or off, and in 1992 deaths were down to two. If a dryer falls in water, unplug it first, then reach in. Simplified illustration, parts enlarged; generic plug, no brand.
