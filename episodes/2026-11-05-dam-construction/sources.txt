Dam construction (concrete gravity / arch-gravity dam): claims -> sources (verified 2026-11-02 from search excerpts; usbr.gov page fetches are blocked in the cloud run, so excerpts were cross-checked across sources; weak or single-source details dropped)
- Before building a dam across a river, the river is diverted around the site through tunnels in the canyon walls; at Hoover Dam four diversion tunnels were driven through the walls of Black Canyon, two on each side (combined length about 3 miles, not said in the VO):
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/tunnels.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Dam ; https://www.fjdynamics.com/blog/industry-insights-65/how-are-dams-built-613
- A cofferdam of earth and rock was dumped into the river just below the tunnel inlets; 24 hours after it was started it was high enough to block the channel and force the river through the tunnels ("within a day"); a second cofferdam below the dam site kept the river from backing into the work area:
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/coffer.html ; https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/tunnels.html (Bureau history excerpt) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofferdam
- The work area between the cofferdams is dewatered, then loose material and weathered rock are excavated down to sound bedrock:
  https://www.fjdynamics.com/blog/industry-insights-65/how-are-dams-built-613 ; https://dnrweblink.state.co.us/CWCB/0/doc/69323/Page41.aspx ; https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/coffer.html
- Deep holes are drilled into the foundation rock and grout (cement slurry) is pumped in to form a grout curtain against seepage under the dam (Hoover: a single line of holes 100-125 ft deep, not said):
  https://web.mst.edu/rogersda/hoover_dam/Grout%20Curtain%20Failure-Hoover%20Dam.pdf ; https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/foundation-and-galleries-in-gravity-dam/238359034 ; https://www.usbr.gov/tsc/techreferences/research/GR8603.pdf
- Concrete heats up as it cures; Reclamation engineers calculated that Hoover Dam poured in one continuous pour would have taken about 125 years to cool, and the stresses would have cracked it:
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/concrete.html ; https://www.spacedaily.com/s-hoover-dam-was-cast-in-columns-because-one-solid-pour-would-take-125-years-to-reach-air-temperature/ ; https://www.nrmca.org/america250/hoover-dam/
- So the dam was built as separate blocks (columns) poured in lifts; each lift had 1-inch steel pipes, river water then refrigerated water circulated through them to remove the heat; about 582 miles of pipe; when a block stopped contracting, the pipes and the gaps between blocks were filled with grout so the blocks act as one structure:
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/concrete.html ; https://www.letsbuild.com/blog/how-they-built-the-hoover-dam ; https://www.maxtour.co/how-did-they-cool-the-concrete-at-hoover-dam/ ; https://www.vozpopuli.com/indux/en/the-concrete-in-hoover-dam-needed-more-than-a-century-to-cool-on-its-own-and-what-engineers-buried-in-the-wall-is-still-there/8305/
- Water pressure increases with depth, so the dam is thin at the top and thick at the base (Hoover: 45 ft at the crest, 660 ft at the base; cut from the VO for length):
  https://user.engineering.uiowa.edu/~fluids/Posting/Video_Gallery/video_ch02/v2_3.html ; https://shpo.nv.gov/uploads/documents/81000382.pdf ; https://structurae.net/en/structures/hoover-dam
- Afterwards the diversion tunnels are closed: at Hoover concrete plugs were placed in two tunnels in 1934 and the other two became outlets for the spillways and penstocks ("plugged or turned into outlets"); the river then backs up into the reservoir:
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/History/essays/tunnels.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Dam
- CTA (walking across a dam that tall) is only a question.
