## YouTube description
How It's Built #40
A garbage disposal has no blades. Under the sink drain sits a steel grinding chamber with a flat spinning plate at the bottom; on many models the motor turns it at about 1,700 rpm. Two small, blunt metal lugs (impellers) swivel on the plate and fling food outward against the grind ring, a stationary ring with sharp teeth and narrow slots. The food is torn apart against the teeth, a bit like a cheese grater, and only bits small enough to pass the slots escape, flushed down the drain by the running water. That is why ice cubes can't "sharpen the blades": there is nothing to sharpen, though ice can scrub gunk off the ring. Run cold water so grease stays solid and gets chopped up; hot water melts it, and it can harden again further down the pipe. If it hums but won't spin, it's jammed: cut the power first, turn the hex socket on the bottom with the wrench that came with it, then press the red reset button. Check your own model's manual. Simplified illustration, parts enlarged; generic disposer, no brand.
