## Caption (all platforms)
Soil isn't solid. It's grains and water, and it squeezes together under weight, which is why a tower on a shallow base can sink and lean like the one in Pisa. So skyscrapers reach down. A drill bores a deep hole, thick slurry holds the walls up, a steel cage drops in, and concrete is pumped in from the bottom, pushing the slurry out. That's one pile, and a tower can need hundreds. Some stand on bedrock. When the rock is too deep, the soil's friction along each pile holds the load. A thick concrete raft ties them all together. Every tall building still sinks a little; the goal is a tiny bit, and evenly. Would you trust friction to hold up a tower?
