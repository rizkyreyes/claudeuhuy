## YouTube description
How It's Built #42
Not cleaning out lint is the leading factor in home dryer fires (U.S. Fire Administration). A heater warms the air and a fan pulls it through your clothes; every load sheds fibers. The lint screen catches a lot of them, but not all: the rest ride the air into the exhaust duct that runs through your wall to the outside, and stick to its sides, worst of all in floppy plastic or foil ducts that sag and kink. As the duct narrows, hot air can't get out and heat builds around the heater. A high-limit thermostat should switch the heat off, but if it fails and the vent is blocked, lint near the heater can ignite, with a duct full of more lint running through the wall. In one USFA study (2008-2010) fire departments responded to about 2,900 home dryer fires a year, peaking in January. Clean the screen every load, use rigid or semi-rigid metal duct, check the outside flap opens while the dryer runs, and if drying takes longer than normal, the duct may be blocked. Simplified illustration; generic dryer, no brand.
