"Lint left in your dryer is the number one reason dryers catch fire."
  - USFA, Clothes Dryer Fires in Residential Buildings (2008-2010), Topical Fire Report Series Vol. 13 Issue 7 (Aug 2012): "Failure to clean (34 percent) was the leading factor contributing to the ignition of clothes dryer fires in residential buildings." https://www.usfa.fema.gov/statistics/reports/fire-causes  (report PDF archived: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120913075423/http://www.usfa.fema.gov/statistics/reports/electrical_and_appliances.shtm)
  - USFA newer page: failure to clean (31%) was the leading factor in home dryer fires 2018-2020. https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/appliance-and-electrical
  - NFPA (2010-2014 data): leading cause of home dryer/washer fires = failure to clean (31%); dust, fiber or lint = leading item first ignited in dryer fires (27%). https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2016/06/f32/NFPA_DryerFactSheet.pdf
  - Wording note: "number one reason" = "leading factor contributing to ignition" (USFA). We do NOT say "most fires" or give a % on screen.
"And the worst of it hides inside your wall" / "The rest ride the hot air into the duct, the pipe that runs through your wall to the outside." / "Lint sticks to the sides"
  - CPSC consumer tips (Overheated clothes dryers can cause fires, pub. 5022): fires can occur when lint builds up in the dryer or in the exhaust duct. https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/5022.pdf
  - CPSC staff evaluation (2003, electric dryers / lint ignition): lint may still accumulate inside a dryer even with properly vented ducting and the lint screen cleaned before each load. https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080925080307/https:/www.cpsc.gov/library/foia/foia03/os/dryer.pdf
"The screen catches a lot of them, but not all." -> same CPSC 2003 evaluation (lint still accumulates past a cleaned screen).
"especially in floppy foil ducts that sag and kink"
  - CPSC 5022: most manufacturers specify rigid or corrugated semi-rigid metal duct (maximum airflow); flexible plastic or foil ducts more easily trap lint and can be kinked or crushed, sharply cutting airflow. https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/5022.pdf
"Load after load, the pipe gets narrower. Now the hot air can't get out. Heat builds up around the heater."
  - CPSC 5022 (buildup restricts airflow, overheats the dryer); CPSC 2003 tests: with the exhaust vent 75 or 100 percent blocked, temperatures inside the dryer rose significantly. URLs above.
"A safety thermostat should cut the power. But if it fails, and the vent is blocked, the lint near the heater can catch fire."
  - CPSC report on dryer heater housings: "Lint that accumulates on the heater housing can easily ignite under conditions of a failed high-limit thermostat and a blocked exhaust vent." https://webharvest.gov/congress115th/20190109020243/https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/ClothesDryersHeaterHousings.pdf
  - High-limit thermostat near/within the heater housing cycles the element off when airflow stops (same CPSC material).
"And that fire has a pipe full of fuel, running right through your wall."
  - Lint is the fuel: NFPA (dust, fiber, lint = leading item first ignited) + CPSC (lint builds up in the exhaust duct), duct runs through the wall (CPSC 5022 installation guidance). The spread along the duct is shown as illustration; no speed/temperature claimed.
"In one federal study, fire departments faced about two thousand nine hundred home dryer fires a year, and they peak in January."
  - USFA 2012 report (2008-2010 data): "An estimated 2,900 clothes dryer fires in residential buildings are reported to U.S. fire departments each year and cause an estimated 5 deaths, 100 injuries, and $35 million in property loss." "Clothes dryer fire incidence in residential buildings was higher in the fall and winter months, peaking in January at 11 percent." https://www.usfa.fema.gov/statistics/reports/fire-causes ; press release https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120913075508/http://www.usfa.fema.gov/media/press/2012releases/080712.shtm
  - Note: other estimates are higher (CPSC ~15,500/yr; USFA 2002-2004 ~12,700/yr) because they count differently, so the VO says "in one federal study" and "about".
"So clean the screen every single load."
  - CPSC 5022: clean the lint screen/filter before or after each use; Montgomery County MD Fire & Rescue: clean before or after drying each load. https://montgomerycountymd.gov/mcfrs-info/tips/citizens/dryer.html
"Use a rigid metal duct, not plastic or foil." -> CPSC 5022 (above).
"Go outside while it runs, and check that the flap opens."
  - CPSC 5022: check the outside vent while the dryer is operating; if exhaust air isn't escaping, the vent or duct may be blocked. URL above.
"If your clothes take longer to dry, the duct might be blocked."
  - Fire-service dryer tips (CPSC-derived): clothes still damp after a normal cycle or taking longer than normal to dry = lint screen or exhaust duct may be blocked. https://montgomerycountymd.gov/mcfrs-info/tips/citizens/dryer.html
