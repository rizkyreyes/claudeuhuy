suspension-bridge-p2 (3d-19), facts checked 8 Nov 2026 (cloud run, search excerpts; goldengate.org page fetches are blocked in the cloud network,
its text came through search results). Every claim has the official Golden Gate Bridge District page or two agreeing sources.
Cut / not claimed: that the Golden Gate deck itself was lifted from barges (it wasn't shown as such; the VO says "on modern bridges"),
the $130,000 net cost, the number of deck sections (only one secondary source: 747), tower movement while the deck goes on, cable sag numbers.

The deck (road) of a suspension bridge is not held up from below: it hangs from the main cables on vertical suspender ropes
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/how-the-bridge-spans-the-golden-gate/ (from part 1: roadway hangs from the cables)
  - https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=176573 (historical marker: once towers, anchorages and main cables are in place, no temporary supports are needed under the bridge; deck sections each hung from the suspenders)
Hundreds of feet above the sea (Golden Gate: 220 ft clearance above mean higher high water)
  - https://goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gate_Bridge (infobox: clearance below 220 ft)
Main cables were spun in part 1 (recap)
  - see episodes/2026-11-11-suspension-bridge-p1/sources.txt
Cable bands are clamped around the main cables every 50 feet on the Golden Gate; the vertical suspender ropes hang from the cable bands
  - https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats/ ("Main Cable bands are located every 50 feet along the main cables and the vertical suspender ropes are hung from the cable bands")
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/steel-suspender-rope-exhibit/
From each band a pair of suspender ropes; 250 pairs on the Golden Gate
  - https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats/ ("250 pairs of vertical suspender ropes that are spaced 50 feet apart")
  - https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/bridge-projects/major-bridge-improvements/ (all 250 pairs replaced in the 1970s)
Once towers, anchorages and cables are up, the road needs no support underneath (no temporary supports)
  - https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=176573
On modern bridges deck sections arrive by barge; a gantry crane riding on the main cables lifts each section straight up (moves over the cables "like a train car on rails" when unloaded)
  - https://enr.com/articles/30840-tacoma-narrows-decksare-lifted-into-place (Tacoma Narrows 2007: sections off a barge, eight gantries riding on the main cables)
  - https://www.bridgeweb.com/First-deck-lift-takes-place-at-Tacoma-Narrows/979
  - https://ingenia.org.uk/articles/bridging-the-eurasian-gap/ (gantry clamped to the main cables, rolls along them between lifts)
  - https://dormanlongtechnology.com/en/projects/Jiangyin.htm (deck units lifted by gantries mounted on the cables, strand jacks)
The hangers are hooked on (each section hung from the suspenders), the crane moves on to the next position
  - https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=176573 ("each section hung from the suspenders")
  - https://enr.com/articles/30840-tacoma-narrows-decksare-lifted-into-place
The sections grow out from the towers in a balanced way until they meet in the middle, then form one continuous road
  - https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=176573 ("built in sections, proceeding out from the towers in a balanced way"; sections met in the middle)
On the Golden Gate a safety net under the work caught 19 workers (the "Half Way to Hell Club")
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Way_to_Hell_Club
  - https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/goldengate-workers
  - https://www.history.co.uk/articles/golden-gate-bridge-construction-workers-net
Load path: car -> hangers (suspenders) -> main cables -> over the tower tops -> anchorages
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/how-the-bridge-spans-the-golden-gate/ (cables carry roadway + traffic to the tower tops)
  - part 1 sources (anchorages hold the cable ends)
