1. The small door at the bottom front of a front-load washer covers the drain pump filter (debris filter / pump filter).
   - Samsung support "How do I clean the debris filter in my front loading Samsung washing machine?" (pump filter access panel near the bottom front) https://www.samsung.com/ie/support/home-appliances/how-do-i-clean-the-debris-filter-in-my-front-loading-samsung-washing-machine
   - GE Appliances "Front Load Washer - Cleaning a Pump Filter" (access cover at the bottom front, opened with a coin or flat screwdriver) https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16687
   - Simplification: on some Whirlpool models the filter sits behind the bottom drawer instead (producthelp.whirlpool.com "Cleaning the Drain Pump Filter"); VO says "your front-load washer", generic washer on screen.
2. "Ignore it, and the washer can stop draining": LG US help "OE error code front load washer": OE = the washer was unable to drain; main causes a clogged drain pump filter, kinked drain hose or clogged standpipe. https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/oe-error-code-front-load-washer-CT10000010-1337714738535
   Sears Home Services "How does a washing machine drain?": clean the filter trap so blockages don't trigger back-ups and leaks. https://searshomeservices.com/blog/how-does-a-washing-machine-drain
   "leaving your clothes in dirty water" = the drum is not emptied when the drain fails (the OE/F9 E1 pages are about water left in the washer). Illustrative.
3. "At the end of every wash and rinse, an electric pump switches on": Sears Home Services "How does a washing machine drain?": "This electric pump activates at the end of each wash or rinse cycle to move water out of the drum and through the drain hose." https://searshomeservices.com/blog/how-does-a-washing-machine-drain
4. "spinning impeller pushes the water out of the drum, up the drain hose, into your drain pipe": same Sears page ("The drain pump typically has an impeller, which spins to create enough force to move water up and out."); drain hose into a standpipe/drainpipe: LG OE page (standpipe) and Whirlpool F9 E1 page (drain hose into the drainpipe, max 4.5 in) https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Front_Load_Washers/Error_Codes/%22F%22_Error_Codes/F9_E1_-_Front_Load_Washer
5. "coins, buttons, bits of plastic, and loads of lint" + "That junk can chew up the impeller" + "in front of the pump sits a plastic strainer":
   Sears Home Services: "The pump contains a filter screen trapping debris like coins, buttons, plastic, etc. This filter trap prevents erosion damage to the impeller."; "Debris can damage the impeller over time." https://searshomeservices.com/blog/how-does-a-washing-machine-drain
   Samsung AU "Cleaning the pump filter": catches lost items such as coins and collects lint. https://www.samsung.com/au/support/home-appliances/how-to-clean-the-pump-filter/
   "plastic strainer": the removable filter is a plastic cartridge with a knob (LG/Samsung/Whirlpool photos and steps: twist the filter counterclockwise to remove it). Slot shape illustrative.
6. "It's also the lint filter": Samsung: "the pump filter can also be seen as the lint filter for front load washers and washer/dryer combos." https://www.samsung.com/au/support/home-appliances/how-to-clean-the-pump-filter/
7. "Over months, it packs tight. The water slows to a trickle, and the drain takes too long." = clogging reduces drainage (LG OE page; Sears back-ups). "packs tight" and "trickle" are plain-language descriptions, no number.
8. "On some Whirlpool washers, a drain over eight minutes throws an error code": Whirlpool "F9 E1 - Front Load Washer": F9 E1 indicates drain times longer than expected, typically over eight (8) minutes. https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Front_Load_Washers/Error_Codes/%22F%22_Error_Codes/F9_E1_-_Front_Load_Washer
   On screen: "ERROR CODE" only, no real code letters or brand logo.
9. "lay down towels and a shallow pan": Whirlpool pump filter page: set a shallow pan and towels underneath first; it is normal to catch about a cup of water. LG UK: lay down a towel first. https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Laundry/Washers/Product_Info/Washer_Cleaning_and_Care/Cleaning_the_Pump_Filter_-_Front_Load_Washer
10. "Water waits down there, and after a hot cycle it can burn": Whirlpool (normal to catch about a cup of water); Samsung SG: if you clean the filter right after a hot cycle, hot water may flow out; Samsung: allow one hour so the water can cool; LG CA/UAE: water can burn. https://www.samsung.com/sg/support/home-appliances/how-to-clean-the-debris-filter-in-samsung-washing-machine
11. "Many have a small hose to drain it": Samsung CA "How to clean your Samsung washer" (water drained from the emergency hose before removing the filter) https://www.samsung.com/ca/support/home-appliances/samsung-washer-clean/ ; LG: let the remaining water drain from the hose.
12. "Twist the filter counterclockwise, pull it out, rinse it in warm water": LG US "How to clean the drain pump filter": twist the filter counterclockwise to remove it, clean it with a soft brush and warm water, reinstall it securely. https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-washer-how-to-clean-the-drain-pump-filter-CT10000010-20150206838321 ; Whirlpool: turn counterclockwise to remove, reinstall clockwise and tight.
13. "Check the impeller spins freely": Samsung: make sure the drain pump propeller behind the debris filter is not blocked. (Samsung IE page above.)
14. "Screw it back in tight, or it will leak": Whirlpool: reinstall clockwise and tight; LG: reinstall securely. Leaking from a loose filter cap = reason for "tight" (plain consequence of an unsealed drain path).
15. "LG says every month": LG US help: "It is recommended to clean the drain pump filter on a monthly basis." https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-washer-how-to-clean-the-drain-pump-filter-CT10000010-20150206838321
16. "Samsung says five or six times a year": Samsung AU: clean the pump filter 5-6 times a year. https://www.samsung.com/au/support/home-appliances/how-to-clean-the-pump-filter/
    (Maytag "every few months or as needed" also exists: makers differ, so the VO names each maker with its own number.)
