## YouTube description
How It's Built #39
The small door at the bottom of a front-load washer covers the drain pump filter. At the end of every wash and rinse, an electric drain pump switches on; its spinning impeller pushes the water out of the drum, up the drain hose and into the drain pipe. A plastic strainer in front of the pump stops coins, buttons, bits of plastic and lint before they can damage the impeller; on front loaders it is also the lint filter. Over months it packs tight, the drain slows, and the washer can stop draining (some Whirlpool washers show an error when a drain takes over eight minutes). To clean it: towels and a shallow pan first, the water behind the door can be hot after a hot cycle, drain it with the small emergency hose if yours has one, twist the filter counterclockwise, pull it out, rinse it in warm water, check that the impeller spins freely, and screw it back in tight. LG recommends cleaning it every month, Samsung five or six times a year; check your own manual. Simplified illustration, parts enlarged; generic washer, no brand.
