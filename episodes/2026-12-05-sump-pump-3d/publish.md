## YouTube description
How It's Built #46
Many basements have a sump pit: a hole in the floor that collects the groundwater pushing in after heavy rain or snowmelt. A perforated drain pipe (drain tile) along the footing gathers the water and carries it to the pit at the lowest spot. As the pit fills, a float rises and flips a switch; the pump's impeller throws the water up the discharge pipe and out, away from the foundation. When the level drops, the float falls and the pump shuts off. A one-way check valve keeps the water in the pipe from falling back into the pit, so the pump doesn't re-pump the same water and wear out. Common failures: power outages during storms (that's what battery backup pumps are for), a float stuck on the pit wall, and a discharge line frozen in winter. Typical sump pump life is about 7 to 10 years. A standard homeowners policy usually doesn't cover sump pump overflow unless you add water backup coverage; check your own policy. Simplified illustration: pit, pump and pipes drawn bigger; layouts vary (interior or exterior drain tile, other switch types); generic house.
