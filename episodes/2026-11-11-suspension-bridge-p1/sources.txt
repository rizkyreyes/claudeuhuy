suspension-bridge-p1 (3d-18), facts checked 7 Nov 2026 (cloud run, search excerpts; pbs.org and most page fetches are blocked in the cloud network).
Every claim in the script has at least two agreeing sources or the official Golden Gate Bridge District page. Cut: anchorage weight
(only an unconfirmed 1930s figure), who invented aerial spinning, the kite flyer's name/age (sources disagree on the age), cable length (7,650 vs 7,659 ft).

Each Golden Gate main cable contains 27,572 galvanized steel wires (61 strands of ~452 wires)
  - https://goldengate.org/exhibits/cross-section-of-a-main-cable ("Main Cable Statistics")
  - https://goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats
  - https://marinmagazine.com/?p=3903 (452 x 61 = 27,572)
The cables were spun in place, wire by wire (aerial spinning), not lifted as finished cables
  - https://goldengate.org/exhibits/spinning-the-main-cables
  - https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/goldengate-spinning
The deck (and every car on it) hangs from the main cables by suspender ropes; the cables carry roadway + traffic to the tower tops
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/how-the-bridge-spans-the-golden-gate/
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/facts-and-figures-about-the-bridge/
The cables pull with huge force; each end is anchored in a massive concrete anchorage that keeps it from moving
  - https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/suspension-bridges
  - https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats ("weight of the Bridge, not counting the concrete anchorages")
Steel eyebars are embedded (cast) in the anchorage concrete; each wire bundle is secured to one of them
  - https://farisalmahdawi.blogspot.com/2009/01/suspension-bridge-information_17.html (catwalk and hauling system / anchorage eyebars)
  - https://www.mfgrobots.com/Article/mfg/mpm/2264.html
Order of work: piers and anchorages, then towers and saddles, then catwalk cables and catwalk, then spinning
  - https://centros.edu.xunta.gal/cafi/aulavirtual/pluginfile.php/40113/mod_imscp/content/1/construction_sequence_on_bridges.html
  - https://farisalmahdawi.blogspot.com/2009/01/suspension-bridge-information_17.html
A steel saddle on top of each tower carries (cradles) the main cable
  - https://centros.edu.xunta.gal/cafi/aulavirtual/pluginfile.php/40113/mod_imscp/content/1/construction_sequence_on_bridges.html ("Erect towers and saddles")
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/how-the-bridge-spans-the-golden-gate/ (cables place the load on the tower tops)
At Niagara in 1848 the first line across the gorge for the suspension bridge was carried by a kite (kite-flying contest, January 1848)
  - https://history.nebraska.gov/publications/homan-j-walsh-and-kite-helped-build-bridge
  - https://www.discoverniagara.org/homan-walsh-and-his-kite
  - https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Niagara_Suspension_Bridge
The thin first line pulls heavier ropes, until the catwalk (a walkway under each cable) can be hung
  - https://history.nebraska.gov/publications/homan-j-walsh-and-kite-helped-build-bridge (string -> heavier rope -> cable)
  - https://farisalmahdawi.blogspot.com/2009/01/suspension-bridge-information_17.html (catwalks below each main cable for spinning access)
A spinning wheel travels along the catwalk from one anchorage over the towers to the other, pulling a loop of wire; the loop is fixed
at the far end (strand shoe) and the wheel goes back; repeated thousands of times
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/spinning-the-main-cables/
  - https://farisalmahdawi.blogspot.com/2009/01/suspension-bridge-information_17.html
On the Golden Gate, spinning took about six months (October 1935 to May 1936; official: 6 months and 9 days)
  - https://goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats
  - https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/goldengate-spinning
80,000 miles of wire in the main cables
  - https://goldengate.org/exhibits/cross-section-of-a-main-cable
  - https://www.mentalfloss.com/geography/wanderlust/20-awesome-facts-about-golden-gate-bridge
Wires bundled into 61 strands, then compacted into one round cable, 36 3/8 inches in diameter (said as "about three feet thick")
  - https://goldengate.org/exhibits/cross-section-of-a-main-cable
  - https://farisalmahdawi.blogspot.com/2009/01/suspension-bridge-information_17.html ("compact cable and temporarily clamp")
The catwalk is hundreds of feet above the sea (towers 746 ft above water; the catwalk follows the cable up to the tower tops)
  - https://www.goldengate.org/exhibits/facts-and-figures-about-the-bridge/
  - https://goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats

Illustrative in the animation (not claims): bridge generic (not a model of the Golden Gate), spans shortened, towers 72 units,
cable drawn ~2x thick, 9 eyebars instead of one per strand, one wheel per cable (the Golden Gate ran several), one hex pack of 61 strands,
the wire pattern on the strand faces is a texture (not 452 counted wires), the kite crosses the strait in the animation (it crossed
the Niagara gorge in 1848), the montage compresses six months into five seconds.
