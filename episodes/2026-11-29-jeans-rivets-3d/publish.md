## YouTube description
How It's Built #36
Why jeans have copper rivets: the corner of a pocket opening is where the seam ends, so every pull on the pocket piles onto the last few stitches until they snap and the pocket tears. Reno tailor Jacob Davis, who already used copper rivets on horse-blanket straps, set one at each pocket corner. A rivet is a post pushed through every layer of denim from the inside and a cap on the outside, crushed together so the pull goes into metal instead of thread. Davis couldn't afford the patent fee, so he teamed up with his cloth supplier, Levi Strauss; the patent was granted May 20, 1873. The tiny pocket inside the right front pocket was made for a pocket watch. Simplified illustration: generic jeans, rivet cross-section enlarged, no people shown.
