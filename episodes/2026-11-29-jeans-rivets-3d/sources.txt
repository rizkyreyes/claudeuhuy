# jeans-rivets-3d (3d-36) - claim -> source. Checked 21 Nov 2026 (cloud run) via search-engine excerpts (levistrauss.com, britannica.com and wikipedia.org are not fetched directly from the sandbox).

"Those little copper dots on your jeans were patented in eighteen seventy-three." / "They got the patent on May twentieth, eighteen seventy-three."
  -> US Patent 139,121 "Improvement in fastening pocket-openings", J. W. Davis, assignor to himself and Levi Strauss & Co., dated May 20, 1873 (application filed Aug 9, 1872) https://patents.google.com/patent/US139121
  -> National Archives, Prologue blog "Forever in Blue Jeans...and in Court" (grant dated May 20, 1873; a metal rivet at each edge of the pocket opening to prevent ripping of the seam) https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2010/05/20/forever-in-blue-jeansand-in-court/
  -> HISTORY.com "Levi Strauss and Jacob Davis receive patent for blue jeans" (May 20, 1873, work pants reinforced with metal rivets) https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/levi-strauss-and-jacob-davis-receive-patent-for-blue-jeans
"They're holding your pockets together." / "he set one at each pocket corner" -> patent text: rivet or eyelet at each edge (corner) of the pocket opening to keep the seam from ripping (Google Patents US139121; NARA Prologue)
"Without them, the corner of a work-pants pocket was the first thing to rip" -> same patent purpose (prevent ripping at those points); Smithsonian NMAH object 371609 (Davis reinforced pocket openings with copper rivets after a customer's pockets kept tearing) https://www.si.edu/object/nmah_371609
"and everything inside fell out" = what a ripped-open pocket does (illustration; no claim about a specific person or item)
"the pull runs along the opening, but the seam just stops at the corner. So the force piles onto the last few stitches"
  -> pocket corners/edges are the stress points where hand movement and seams meet and stitches break (Seamwork "Strong and pretty pockets" https://www.seamwork.com/articles/strong-and-pretty-pockets ; US patent 6,138,595 on garment pocket wear: stress concentrates at a fixed tie point at the pocket corner and pulls a hole https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6138595 ; Wikipedia "Bar tack": reinforcement at stress points such as pocket corners https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_tack). General mechanics, no numbers.
"A Reno tailor named Jacob Davis had a customer whose work pants kept tearing like that."
  -> Wikipedia "Jacob W. Davis" (Reno, Nevada tailor; a customer/woodcutter's pants kept wearing out) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_W._Davis ; NPR 21 May 2023 "Durable and enduring, blue jeans turn 150" (a customer whose work pants kept tearing) https://www.npr.org/2023/05/21/1177391804/blue-jeans-150-years-old ; Smithsonian Magazine "The origin of blue jeans" https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/the-origin-of-blue-jeans-89612175/
  Dropped: who ordered the pants (the customer's wife vs the woodcutter himself), the year of the request (1870 vs Jan 1871), the $3 price: sources disagree.
"Davis already used copper rivets to fasten straps on horse blankets."
  -> Levi Strauss & Co. history (via search excerpt): Davis used copper rivets to attach straps to the horse blankets he made; Wikipedia Jacob W. Davis (tents, horse blankets, wagon covers) ; Levi Strauss & Co. PDF "Jacob Davis: His Life and Contributions" https://www.levistrauss.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/Jacob-Davis-His-Life-and-Contributions1.pdf
"A rivet has two parts: a post pushed through every layer of denim from the inside, and a cap on the outside. One hit of the hammer crushes them together"
  -> jeans rivets are two-piece fasteners: a post pushed through the fabric from the inside and a cap on the outside; a setting tool struck with a hammer drives the post into the cap and crimps it (Sew4Home "How to install metal rivets" https://sew4home.com/how-to-attach-metal-rivets-on-sewing-projects/ ; Minerva Patterns "Bartacks, rivets and reinforcement details": a rivet is a two-part metal fastening, a cap and a post, that clamps the fabric mechanically at the corners of jean pockets https://minervapatterns.com/blog/bartacks-rivets-and-reinforcement-details). Factory jeans set rivets by machine; "one hit of the hammer" describes the hand-set method (as on Davis's bench); the video shows a generic setting punch.
"Now the pull spreads into metal instead of a few threads." -> rivet clamps the fabric mechanically instead of relying on thread at the stress point (Minerva Patterns; NARA patent summary "to prevent the ripping of the seam at those points")
"Davis couldn't afford the patent fee, so he teamed up with the man who sold him his cloth, Levi Strauss."
  -> Mental Floss "Happy birthday, blue jeans" (Davis didn't have the $68 to apply for a patent and sought a business partner) https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/21775/happy-birthday-blue-jeans ; Mercury News 21 May 2023 "Strong jeans" (Strauss paid the $68 fee) https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-mercury-news/20230521/281891597637686 ; Smithsonian NMAH (Strauss, Davis's cloth supplier, received his proposal in 1872); Levi Strauss & Co. "The history of denim" (Davis bought the cloth from Strauss) https://www.levistrauss.com/our-story/the-history-of-denim/
  The $68 amount is NOT spoken (only "the patent fee").
"And that tiny pocket inside the right front pocket? It was made for a pocket watch, safe from getting smashed."
  -> Levi Strauss & Co. "Pockets full of history" (2017): the small pocket on the right front is called a watch pocket, intended to store a pocket watch; traced to the oldest pair of waist overalls in the archive (1879) https://levistrauss.com/2017/01/12/pockets-full-history/
  -> Britannica "Why do jeans have that tiny pocket?" (feature of the original waist overalls, intended to hold a watch, kept it from getting smashed or lost) https://www.britannica.com/story/why-do-jeans-have-that-tiny-pocket
  Not spoken: the year the watch pocket first appeared (1873 patent drawing vs 1879 oldest surviving pair vs 1890 mass production: sources differ).
