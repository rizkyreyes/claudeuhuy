## Caption (all platforms)
An elevator hangs from at least three steel ropes that together are many times stronger than the load, and a counterweight does most of the lifting. The motor's brake is held shut by springs and only opens with electricity, so a power cut clamps it on. And if every rope snapped? A spinning governor locks its own rope, which yanks steel wedges into the guide rails until the car grinds to a stop. It all goes back to 1854, when Elisha Otis had the rope cut in front of a crowd. Do you trust the elevator, or take the stairs?
