Episode 3d-07 elevator-brakes: claim -> source. Claims locked from concordant search-result excerpts of these pages
(this run could not open publisher pages directly: wikipedia.org and howstuffworks.com gave DNS errors from the cloud box).

- A traction elevator hangs from several parallel steel hoist ropes; codes require at least three for traction elevators
  -> https://www.dir.ca.gov/Title8/3042.html (Cal. Code of Regs. Title 8 §3042: "minimum number of hoisting ropes ... three for traction elevators")
     https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-8/division-1/chapter-4/subchapter-6/group-ii/article-8/section-3042
- The ropes are built many times stronger than the load (minimum factor of safety = rope breaking strength / static load, about 7.6-11.9
  depending on speed; 8.60 for passenger elevators at 200 ft/min). Spoken only as "many times stronger", no number.
  -> https://www.dir.ca.gov/Title8/3042.html (Table 3042E1) ; https://elevatorworld.com/article/building-suspense/
- A counterweight balances the car (car weight plus part of the rated load), so the motor only moves the difference
  -> https://science.howstuffworks.com/transport/engines-equipment/elevator3.htm ; https://www.scribd.com/doc/305720343/basic-elevator-components
- The machine brake is spring-applied and electrically released: springs hold it on, a solenoid/electromagnet pulls it off,
  so losing power sets the brake
  -> https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5228364 ; https://www.hilliardcorp.com/braking-systems-elevator-modernization/ ;
     https://www.kebamerica.com/blog/spring-set-brakes-how-it-works/
- Overspeed governor: a sheave with its own governor rope looped from the machine room to a tension pulley in the pit, connected to the car;
  centrifugal flyweights swing out when the car goes too fast and trip the governor, which grips/stops the governor rope
  -> https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8931598 ; https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9033111 ;
     https://patents.google.com/patent/US9033111
- The stopped governor rope pulls the safety linkage on the moving car, which drives wedges against the guide rails; the car moving down
  relative to the wedges makes them bite harder (self-tightening), so the car stops on the rails without the hoist ropes
  -> https://patents.google.com/patent/US5159995 ; https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP0432634NWB1/document.html ;
     https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/elevator-safety-gear (mechanism only; its numbers are not used)
- Pit buffers stop a car or counterweight that goes past its normal limit: spring buffers (slower elevators) and oil buffers (faster)
  -> https://patents.google.com/patent/US5238088A/en ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US3857555A ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US2744587A/en
- History: in 1854, at the Exhibition of the Industry of All Nations (New York Crystal Palace), mechanic Elisha Otis rode his safety hoist
  platform up high in front of a crowd and had the rope cut; a wagon spring snapped into ratchet teeth on the guide rails and the platform held
  -> https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Otis ; https://www.britannica.com/topic/Elisha-Otis ; https://www.asme.org/topics-resources/content/elisha-graves-otis ;
     https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/72451-first-safety-elevator-lift
