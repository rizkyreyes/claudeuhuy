## YouTube description
How It's Built #47
The dent in the bottom of a wine bottle is called a punt, and it says nothing about how good the wine is. It comes from hand-blown glass: the base came out with a rough seam or a sharp point in the middle, so glassblowers pushed that spot up into the bottle. The bottle then stands on a smooth ring, and the rough part sits out of the way; the dome may also have made the base stronger (sources differ on the exact reasons). Machine-made bottles are tougher, so for most still wine the punt is mostly tradition, and some bottles have none. Sparkling wine is different: a sealed bottle of champagne holds around 5 to 6 atmospheres, about three times the pressure in a car tire. A flat bottom would be a weak spot; the dome helps spread the pressure, and champagne bottles are made of much thicker, heavier glass. A deeper dent doesn't mean better wine (Wine Spectator), and the idea that it catches sediment has never been proven. Simplified illustration: generic bottles, the flat-bottom burst is an illustration, not a real test.
