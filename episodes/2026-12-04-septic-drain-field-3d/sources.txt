"If your home is on septic, the water you flush ends up under your lawn, leaking out of pipes full of holes on purpose."
  - EPA, How Your Septic System Works: a typical system = septic tank + drainfield (soil absorption field); the liquid (effluent) leaves the
    tank into the drainfield, where it is treated and filtered as it drains through the soil. https://www.epa.gov/septic/how-your-septic-system-works
  - WVU / LSU AgCenter / Deschutes County drainfield guides: perforated pipe (typically 4-inch PVC, holes pointing down) in gravel trenches.
    https://actat.wvu.edu/files/d/570a5193-1b32-494b-b12e-ec6ea6002a90/soilabsorption.pdf
    https://faculty.lsu.edu/hayes/files/septic_series/absoprtiontrench.pdf
    https://www.deschutes.org/sites/default/files/fileattachments/community_development/page/775/es-standard_drainfield.pdf
"If they clog, sewage comes back up, into your yard or house."
  - Washington State Dept. of Health: a failure can cause sewage to come to the surface around the tank or drainfield or to back up in pipes
    in the building. https://www.doh.wa.gov/Portals/1/Documents/Pubs/337-153.pdf
  - EPA, What to do if your septic system fails: solids reaching the drainfield clog the pores in the gravel and soil; signs include
    standing water / damp spots, odors, slow drains, backups. https://epa.gov/septic/what-do-if-your-septic-system-fails
"More than one in five American homes run on this."
  - EPA SepticSmart (2026 release + septic overview page): more than one in five U.S. homes/households rely on septic (onsite) systems.
    https://www.epa.gov/septic/learn-about-septic-systems
    https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epas-14th-septicsmart-week-practical-and-proactive-septic-system-maintenance-protects
"Liquid leaves the tank and runs downhill into a small concrete box. One outlet per trench, all dead level, so each trench gets an equal share."
  - Missouri DHSS Onsite Wastewater Owner's Manual: the distribution box has one inlet and one outlet per trench; outlets must be level,
    otherwise the flow splits unequally (gravity system). https://health.mo.gov/book/export/html/5021
  - VDH, How Systems Work: pipe from the distribution box carries effluent into the trench.
    https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/environmental-health/onsite-sewage-water-services-updated/how-systems-work/
  - "Small concrete box": D-boxes are commonly precast concrete (generic illustration; some are plastic, not said).
"Then it flows into pipes about four inches wide, laid flat in trenches of gravel, with the holes pointing down."
  - WVU: perforated pipe typically 4-inch PVC with holes pointing down, covered with gravel.
  - Deschutes County: perforated pipe must be laid level (only the effluent sewer upstream of the box slopes).
  - LSU AgCenter: clean gravel bedding at least 6 in below and 2 in above the pipe.
"It drips out through the gravel into the soil, where the real cleaning happens. The soil is packed with bacteria that feed on what's left."
  - EPA: soil full of bacteria digests most contaminants before the wastewater reaches groundwater; microbes on or near the soil treat the effluent.
  - This Old House (site evaluator): the soil is the real engine of the leach field. https://www.thisoldhouse.com/plumbing/understanding-septic-systems
"Within a few feet, most germs are gone before it reaches the groundwater."
  - USEPA (1980) design manual, cited in onsite pathogen reviews: under unsaturated flow, bacteria can be removed within 0.9-1.2 m (3-4 ft)
    of soil; states require 1.5-4 ft or more of separation to the water table. https://vernonjames.ces.ncsu.edu/microbial-pathogens-and-on-site-soil-treatment-systems
  - EPA: partially treated water filters down through the soil and enters the groundwater. (VO says "most germs", never "all".)
"But those bacteria need air. That's why the trenches are shallow, and why only grass belongs on top. Park a car on it, and crushed soil chokes them."
  - UF/IFAS + extension drainfield guides: oxygen is required by the beneficial bacteria; trenches deep enough to filter but shallow enough to
    let oxygen reach the soil; turfgrass (shallow fibrous roots) is the usual cover; compaction from driving/parking reduces bacterial
    effectiveness and percolation. https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/SS687/pdf  https://shorestewards.cw.wsu.edu/faq/protecting-your-drainfield/
  - Univ. of Nebraska extension: avoid driving or parking vehicles over the drainfield. https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/g1479/2008/pdf/view/g1479-2008.pdf
"Over the years, a black, slimy layer of bacteria grows along the bottom of each trench. It slows the water down."
  - InspectAPedia / WVU soil-absorption guide: the biomat is a black, jelly-like bacterial layer that forms first along the trench bottom near
    the perforations; as it thickens the soil becomes less permeable. https://inspectapedia.com/septic/Septic_Biomat_Formation.php
"Skip pumping the tank, and solids wash out and clog it even faster."
  - EPA: failure to perform routine maintenance (pumping) lets solids migrate from the tank into the drainfield and clog the system.
  - Biomat sources: tank maintenance and effluent filters slow biomat growth (proper organic loading).
"The water has nowhere to go but up. The grass turns bright green and spongy, even in dry weather, and sewage can surface in the yard."
  - WA DOH: bright green, spongy grass over the drainfield, even in dry weather; sewage surfacing.
  - Cornell CWMI: lush grass over the drainfield during dry spells is a symptom. https://www.css.cornell.edu/cwmi/waterquality/septic/CCEWQ-YourSepticSystem-Failure.pdf
