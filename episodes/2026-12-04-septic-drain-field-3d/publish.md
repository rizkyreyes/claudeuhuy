## YouTube description
How It's Built #44
If your home is on septic, the liquid that leaves the tank flows downhill into a small distribution box with one level outlet per trench, so every trench gets an equal share. From there it runs into perforated pipes (typically 4-inch, holes pointing down) laid flat in gravel trenches. It drips out through the gravel into the soil, where bacteria feed on what's left; under unsaturated conditions most germs are removed within a few feet of soil before the water reaches groundwater (U.S. EPA). Those bacteria need oxygen, so trenches are shallow and only grass belongs on top; parking or driving on the field compacts the soil. Over the years a black, slimy biomat grows along the trench bottoms and slows the water; skipping tank pumping lets solids wash out and clog it faster. Warning signs: bright green, spongy grass over the field even in dry weather, soggy spots, sewage surfacing or backing up (Washington State Dept. of Health, EPA). More than 1 in 5 U.S. homes use septic (EPA). Simplified illustration: pipes drawn thicker, layout varies by state code; generic house and car.
