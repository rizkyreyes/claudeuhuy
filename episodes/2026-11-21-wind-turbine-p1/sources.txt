# wind-turbine-p1 (3d-28) sources, checked 15 Nov 2026 (cloud run). energy.gov and oneenergy.com were not reachable from the sandbox (DNS): checked on search excerpts of those pages.

Hook / structure "held up by a buried slab of concrete and the dirt on top" (gravity spread footing, backfill weight resists overturning; a wider footing resists overturning better):
- https://civilrenewables.squarespace.com/blog/gravity-base-foundations-for-wind-turbines  (gravity-base foundations rely on gravity to resist overturning; typically 7-12 ft below surface)
- US patents 10968894 / 10851763 (image-ppubs.uspto.gov): slab held by bearing on the subgrade + weight of the backfill on top; backfill weight counteracts the overturning moment; larger diameter resists overturning better
- https://www.windpowerengineering.com/take-a-closer-look-at-pouring-turbine-foundations/  (conventional foundations: large concrete pads ~20 m across, 2-3 m deep)
  Wording "This wind turbine" = the one in our animation (a gravity footing); not claimed for every turbine (some use piles or rock anchors).

"wind tries to tip the whole tower over" = overturning moment (same patents / gravity-base article).

"cage of steel rebar, as much as fifty tons":
- Knox County, Ohio windfarm picture tour (knoxcountyne.gov/pdfs/planning_zoning/Windfarm_Picture_Tour.pdf): 48 tons of rebar, 495 cubic yards per foundation
- South Dakota PUC EL17-055 appendix A (puc.sd.gov): V80 78 m spread footing ~505 cu yd concrete, ~54 tons Grade 60 rebar
- One Energy (oneenergy.com/?p=15041): ~300 cu yd, 40,000-60,000 lb rebar (smaller machines) -> hence "as much as" and "often around"

"ring of anchor bolts, more than a hundred":
- One Energy (oneenergy.com/?p=14455): 1.5 MW turbine uses 120 anchor bolts at the base
- Enerpac time-lapse blog (blog.enerpac.com/wind-turbine-construction-time-lapse-video/): base tower bolted to the foundation with 160 bolts

"trucks pour concrete, often around five hundred cubic yards": Knox County 495 cu yd, SD PUC ~505 cu yd (above); larger machines 850-900 cu yd (DNV, https://www.dnv.com/article/foundation-concrete-voids-229998/)

"dirt goes back on top" once the concrete has set: gravity-base article + patents above (backfill placed after the concrete sets)

Tower "arrives in giant steel sections", crane lowers the first over the anchor bolts, crews tighten the nuts, each section bolts onto the one below (flanges):
- Enerpac blog (above): base section bolted to the foundation with bolt tensioners, mid-tower sections bolted with torque wrenches
- https://www.liebherr.com/en-cy/group/magazine/wind-day/onshore-offshore-assembly-5040545 ; cleantechnica.com/2022/12/13/how-are-wind-turbines-made/ (towers in sections, lifted by crane and bolted)

"the average new US tower puts the hub about three hundred forty feet in the air":
- energy.gov (DOE WETO, Land-Based Wind Market Report summary): average hub height of new US land-based turbines ~103.4 m (~339 ft) in 2023 -> "about 340 ft"
  (a secondary summary quotes 98.1 m; we follow the DOE page)

"the nacelle, the machine room on top" + "on a smaller turbine, about fifty tons":
- US patent 7883131 / KHL / Reuters Events (via search): ~1.5 MW class nacelle ~50 tons incl. generator, gearbox and housing; a 3 MW nacelle ~80 t without drivetrain -> "on a smaller turbine, about fifty tons"
- "the big lift": wording only. NOT "the heaviest lift": a bottom tower section can weigh up to ~80 t (US patent search excerpt), more than a ~50 t nacelle, so "heaviest" was cut and re-recorded.

"blades, each over two hundred feet long": energy.gov "Top 10 things you didn't know about wind power": blades average 210 ft; 2023 average rotor diameter 438 ft (energy.gov "Wind turbines: bigger, better")

"Some crews lift them one at a time. Others bolt all three to the hub on the ground and raise the whole rotor": lancashirebusinessview / Enerpac / Liebherr (rotor assembled on the ground and lifted as one, or blades lifted individually). Both methods are in use.

Cut / not used: exact foundation diameter and depth (varies), total bolts per turbine (25,000 per a vendor), crane capacity (650 t example), curing time.
Illustrative in the 3D: generic turbine, 103 m hub, 64 m blades, 19 m footing, rebar and bolt counts drawn fewer and thicker than real.
