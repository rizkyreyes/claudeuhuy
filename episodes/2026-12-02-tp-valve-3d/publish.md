## YouTube description
How It's Built #41
A tank water heater has one last safety device: the temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve. The thermostat normally cuts the heat at the temperature you set. If it fails, the heat keeps climbing, the water expands and the pressure rises, and under pressure water can get hotter than 212°F without boiling (superheated). If the tank then splits, that water flashes into steam, well over a thousand times its volume, all at once. The T&P valve watches both: a probe sits in the top 6 inches of the tank, where the water is hottest; at about 210°F its element expands and lifts the disc off its seat, letting hot water out before it can superheat. A spring holds the same disc shut until about 150 psi, then the water pushes it open. Either way it drains down a discharge pipe that ends near the floor. Lift the test lever once a year (hot water should gush out), never cap that pipe, and if it drips, call a plumber. Ratings and rules vary by valve and local code; check your manual.
