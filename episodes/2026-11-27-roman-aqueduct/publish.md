## YouTube description
How It's Built #34
How Roman aqueducts moved water with only gravity: at the Pont du Gard (southern France, built around 50 AD for Nîmes) the water on top drops less than an inch across the whole bridge. The springs were barely higher than the city, so the channel winds about 30 miles, falling a tiny bit the whole way. Most of it runs underground in a stone channel lined with waterproof mortar of lime and crushed tile; arches only appear where a valley gets in the way. Three tiers lift the channel about 160 ft above the river, some stones weigh six tons and are stacked with no mortar. Deeper valleys got sealed lead pipes (inverted siphons). At the city a round tank split the flow into ten pipes. Limestone in the water coated the channel and crews chipped it out every few years. Simplified illustration: generic three-tier bridge, map not to scale, siphon and distribution tank are generic.
