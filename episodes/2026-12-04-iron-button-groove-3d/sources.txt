1. The notch near the tip of the soleplate is a button groove; buttons settle into the gap between the plate and the rest of the iron, so you can
   press the fabric around them without melting/flattening them. LOCKED
   - Hunker, "Your Iron May Have A Little-Known Feature..." https://www.hunker.com/2085860/what-is-button-groove-on-iron-for/ (excerpt: "the notch creates some separation between the plate and the rest of the machine. Buttons can settle into that gap")
   - Philips GC160/22 product page ("Button groove speeds up ironing along buttons and seams") https://www.philips.com.sg/shop/SG_myphilipspromo/household-products/ironing/GC160-22/p/GC160_22
   - Panasonic NI-U600 (button groove) https://panasonic.com/ca/consumer/home-appliances/irons/steam/ni-u600.html
   - Patent US8166682B2, soleplate unit: shallow groove on the underside of the front edge, "convenient for ironing the face fabric near the button" https://patents.google.com/patent/US8166682B2/en
   Script: "a shallow groove cut along the front edge, between the hot plate and the body of the iron" / "the button slips into that gap" / "sits out of the heat, while the plate presses the fabric right up to its edge".
2. Plastic buttons can melt under a hot iron; iron around buttons, not over them (melt / crack / burn marks). LOCKED
   - House Digest https://www.housedigest.com/1968368/how-to-iron-clothes-with-buttons-laundry ("plastic buttons can melt under high heat"; "may cause them to crack or develop burn marks")
   - Consumer Reports https://www.consumerreports.org/steam-irons/how-to-iron-a-dress-shirt ; Hunker (above)
   Script: "it can melt, crack, or scorch" (scorch = burn marks).
3. In most irons the heating element is cast into an aluminum soleplate. LOCKED ("In most irons" hedge)
4. Thermostat = a bimetallic element on the soleplate that bends with heat and periodically cuts the current to the heater, then reconnects. LOCKED
   Both from one patent search (26 Nov); the excerpts quoted: "Soleplate 10 can conveniently be cast aluminum with electric heating element 18
   cast in position for uniform heat distribution"; "a bimetallic element 23 secured to the soleplate in cantilever fashion, which periodically
   cuts current to the heater"; "sheathed heater element 33 cast in the soleplate 11". Results of that search (which quote is in which patent
   not confirmed, page reads blocked): https://patents.google.com/patent/US3711972A/en , https://patents.google.com/patent/US3368294A/en ,
   https://patents.google.com/patent/US4285145 , https://patents.google.com/patent/US5367799
   Also: search summary "an electric resistance element is cast into or welded to an aluminum soleplate" (patent results incl. US3599357A).
5. Water tank above the soleplate; a valve meters water in drips into a steam chamber in the hot soleplate, where it flashes into steam and leaves
   through the holes in the sole. LOCKED
   - US2483816A, flash type steam iron https://patents.google.com/patent/US2483816 ; US6209239B1 steam chamber https://patents.google.com/patent/US6209239
   - iFixit "Steam Iron - Step 2: How does it work?" https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Steam+Iron+-+Step+2:+How+does+it+work-/132636 (excerpt: heat "turns the water to steam which escapes through the holes in the sole")
6. If the plate is too cold (below the steam setting), the water doesn't turn into steam and leaks/drips from the soleplate. LOCKED
   - Philips support "My Philips steam iron is leaking from its soleplate" https://www.philips.com.au/c-f/XC000013154/my-philips-steam-iron-is-leaking-from-its-soleplate

   - Rowenta (Lowe's listing: anti-drip "prevents leakage when the iron has not reached its optimal temperature") https://www.lowes.com/pd/Rowenta-Iron-with-Auto-Shut-Off/1000212875
