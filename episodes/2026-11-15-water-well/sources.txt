water-well (3d-22), facts checked 10 Nov 2026 (cloud run, from search excerpts; extension.psu.edu is blocked in the cloud network, so its text comes
from search snippets). Every claim below has an agency / extension / engineering source or two agreeing trade sources. Wording kept general where
sources differ.
Cut / not claimed: a required grout depth (states differ: many require only the top 10-20 ft), how deep the pump must sit, pressure-switch settings
(40/60 psi etc.), how often a pump may start, how deep submersibles can go (sources say "up to 400 ft", too vague), well cost, the 43 million people
figure (we use households), "real pumps manage about 25 feet" (cut from the script for length; sources say practical lift is ~20-25 ft).

If you dig deep enough you hit groundwater (water table; below it the sand/gravel is saturated = aquifer)
  - https://www.usgs.gov/publications/depth-groundwater-used-drinking-water-supplies-united-states
  - https://books.gw-project.org/domestic-wells-introduction-and-overview/?p=43 (drilled wells reach the saturated zone)
Suction can't lift water more than ~33 ft (theoretical 33-34 ft at sea level: atmospheric pressure holds up a ~10.3 m / 34 ft water column)
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_(water) (suction lift limited by atmospheric pressure, ~10.3 m of water at sea level)
  - https://www.scienceabc.com/pure-sciences/how-high-can-a-pump-suck-water-10-meter-limit
  - https://www.pumpsandsystems.com/10-factors-affect-suction-lift (practical limit lower, ~20-25 ft; lower at altitude / with warm water)
A suction pump doesn't pull: it lowers the pressure in the pipe and atmospheric pressure on the water pushes it up
  - https://www.scienceabc.com/pure-sciences/how-high-can-a-pump-suck-water-10-meter-limit
  - https://www.pumpsandsystems.com/choosing-right-pump-deep-suction-lift
Built wrong, surface water runs down the outside of the casing into the well (Penn State Extension, quoted from a search excerpt: "surface contamination of individual wells is
usually caused by surface water flowing down the outside of a well casing through the annular space and/or from a loose-fitting or absent well cap";
same fact sheet: coliform bacteria found in many wells)
  - https://extension.psu.edu/sanitary-water-well-construction
  - https://www.thedriller.com/articles/90942-drilling-fluids-proper-grouting-protects-groundwater
Typical home well ~140 ft deep: USGS SIR 2021-5069 median depth of domestic-supply wells = 142 ft (43.3 m); depths vary by aquifer
  - https://www.usgs.gov/publications/depth-groundwater-used-drinking-water-supplies-united-states
Deeper wells use an electric submersible pump placed under the water at the bottom; it pushes water up instead of sucking it
  - https://www.hometips.com/articles/submersible-water-pumps.html ("the pump no longer lifts the water but, rather, pushes it upward")
  - https://www.freshwatersystems.com/blogs/blog/what-is-a-well-pump-and-how-does-it-work
  - https://www.pumpsandsystems.com/choosing-right-pump-deep-suction-lift (submersible avoids the suction-lift limit)
Casing lines the hole so loose material doesn't collapse into it; in sediment the well is cased its whole depth with a screen where water enters
  - https://books.gw-project.org/domestic-wells-introduction-and-overview/?p=43 (drilled well in unconsolidated sediments cased for its entire depth,
    screen or slotted casing where groundwater enters; in bedrock casing keeps loose material from collapsing)
  - https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/pubs/az1504-2015.pdf
A screen with thin slots lets water in and keeps sand out (slot size smaller than the aquifer grains / gravel pack)
  - https://books.gw-project.org/domestic-wells-introduction-and-overview/?p=43
  - https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/extension.arizona.edu/files/pubs/az1504-2015.pdf
The gap between casing and borehole is sealed with grout (cement or bentonite) so surface water can't run down it
  - https://extension.psu.edu/sanitary-water-well-construction
  - https://dnrweblink.state.co.us/CWCB/0/doc/133142/Page46.aspx ("grout prevents movement of ground water and surface water within the annular space")
  - https://www.thedriller.com/articles/90942-drilling-fluids-proper-grouting-protects-groundwater
Without a pressure tank the pump would start every time a tap opens (short cycling), which wears the motor out
  - https://freshwatersystems.com/blogs/blog/how-to-size-a-well-pressure-tank
  - https://midatlanticwater.net/blogs/guides/well-water-pressure-tank-complete-guide
A pressure tank stores water under a cushion of compressed air (air charge / bladder); the pump fills it and the pressure switch shuts the pump off,
then the air pushes the water out to the taps
  - https://inspectapedia.com/water/Water_Tank_Air_Charge.php
  - https://midatlanticwater.net/blogs/guides/well-water-pressure-tank-complete-guide
  - https://skillingsandsons.squarespace.com/blog/how-a-water-well-pressure-tank-works
More than 23 million US households get their drinking water from private wells (EPA)
  - https://www.epa.gov/privatewells (EPA private wells pages: more than 23 million households; DWMAPS private domestic well estimates, 2021; paraphrased from search excerpts)
  - https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/private-domestic-well-estimates-2010
