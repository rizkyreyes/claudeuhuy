## YouTube description
How It's Built #33
How a hydroelectric dam makes electricity: the lake is stored energy, intake towers send it down steel penstocks (Hoover Dam's are 30 ft wide), the water drops about 500 ft, a spiral casing wraps it around the turbine, wicket gates aim it onto the runner, the shaft spins a generator (electromagnets on a 300+ ton rotor inside copper coils), a transformer steps the power up, and the water returns to the river through a widening draft tube. Why the gates can't slam shut: water hammer (a penstock burst in Japan in 1950). Simplified illustration: one turbine unit drawn (Hoover has 17), pipe route simplified.
