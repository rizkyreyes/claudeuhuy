## Caption
The Panama Canal lifts giant ships 85 feet uphill, and it doesn't use a single pump. Ships climb three lock chambers to Gatun Lake. When the valves open, gravity pulls lake water down 18-foot tunnels in the walls and up through holes in the chamber floor, so the water and the ship rise together. Electric locomotives called mules keep the ship centered, sometimes with about two feet to spare on each side. The catch: every crossing sends about 52 million gallons of fresh lake water out to the sea. Would you steer a ship with two feet to spare?
