canal-locks (3d-16), facts checked 6 Nov 2026 (cloud run). en.wikipedia.org and most page fetches are blocked in the cloud network, so search
excerpts were used; every claim below has at least two agreeing sources.

The locks use no pumps: the water moves by gravity alone, from Gatun Lake
  - https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090506171050/http://www.pancanal.com/eng/general/asi-es-el-canal.html (Panama Canal Authority, "This is the Canal": "No pumps are used ... gravity alone")
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks ("The water used to raise and lower vessels in each set of locks comes from Gatun Lake by gravity")
  - https://www.marineinsight.com/guidelines/how-the-water-locks-of-panama-canal-work/
  - https://coms.events/pianc-panama/data/x_abstracts/x_abstract_446.docx (new locks: filling and emptying only by gravity, no pumps)
Gatun Lake is 85 ft (26 m) above sea level; the three steps of the Gatun Locks lift ships 85 ft (25.9 m)
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks
  - https://www.lindahall.org/experience/digital-exhibitions/the-land-divided-the-world-united/08-locks
  - https://www.uflib.ufl.edu/spec/pcm/InterestingFacts.pdf
Ships coming from the Atlantic climb the three-step Gatun flight to the lake (Pacific side: Miraflores 2 steps + Pedro Miguel 1)
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks
  - https://www.britannica.com/topic/Panama-Canal/Locks
Lock chambers: 110 ft wide, 1,050 ft long, usable length 1,000 ft
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks
  - https://panamatourismtravel.blogspot.com/2011/10/panama-canla-physical-characteristic.html (low authority, agrees)
Culverts 18 ft in diameter run lengthwise inside the centre and side walls; water enters/leaves the chamber through holes in the floor
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks
  - https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090506171050/http://www.pancanal.com/eng/general/asi-es-el-canal.html
  - https://www.marineinsight.com/guidelines/how-the-water-locks-of-panama-canal-work/
Valves control the flow; to lower a ship, valves let the water out toward the lower level / the sea
  - https://www.marineinsight.com/guidelines/how-the-water-locks-of-panama-canal-work/
  - https://static.iahr.org/34/540.pdf (valves on the culverts, gravity filling and emptying)
A chamber fills in about 8 minutes (sources: 8 to 10 minutes; script says "about eight")
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks
  - https://www.lindahall.org/experience/digital-exhibitions/the-land-divided-the-world-united/08-locks
Electric locomotives ("mules") on the lock walls hold the ship centred with cables/winches; the ship's own engines push it forward
  - https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Panama_canal_mule
  - https://www.canalmuseum.com/100years/100yearspanamacanal-22.htm
  - https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090506170152/http://www.pancanal.com/eng/projects/locomotive.html
The biggest ships have about 2 ft (60 cm) of water on each side (another source: as little as 12 in; script says "about two feet")
  - https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Panama_canal_mule
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_locks
Each transit uses about 52 million US gallons (~200 million L) of fresh lake water, lost to the sea
  - https://www.uflib.ufl.edu/spec/pcm/InterestingFacts.pdf ("52 million gallons lost to sea per transit")
  - https://wikiwand.com/EN/Panama_Canal
  - https://gcaptain.com/panama-canal-forced-to-balance-shipping-demand-with-need-for-drinking-water (Reuters: ~51 million gallons)
Newer (Neo-Panamax, 2016) locks have water-saving basins beside each chamber that reuse up to 60 % of the water
  - https://core.ac.uk/download/322431879.pdf
  - https://www.FMC.gov/william-p-doyle-surveys-construction-sites-of-expanded-panama-canal-provides-latest-updates/
  - https://coms.events/pianc-panama/data/x_abstracts/x_abstract_446.docx
