## Caption
There's a metal rod inside your water heater that's supposed to rot away. It's the anode rod, usually magnesium, and it corrodes first so the glass-lined steel tank doesn't. Cold water goes in at the top but a dip tube drops it to the bottom, the burner heats it from below, and the exhaust climbs a flue right through the middle of the tank past a twisted baffle. Hot water rises, so the hot pipe pulls from the top. Minerals settle on the bottom over time, which is the popping and rumbling you hear, and flushing the tank clears it out. Have you ever flushed your water heater?
