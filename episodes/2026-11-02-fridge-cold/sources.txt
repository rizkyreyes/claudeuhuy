How a fridge makes cold: claims -> sources (verified 2026-10-30 from search excerpts; sources agree on the cycle, no numbers used)
- A fridge moves heat from inside the cabinet to the room; refrigerant cycles through compressor, condenser, expansion device (capillary tube), evaporator: https://www.danfoss.com/en-us/service-and-support/fix-and-troubleshooting/cooling-installer-hub/the-fridge-how-it-works ; https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/How_a_Refrigerator_Works ; https://www.rtings.com/refrigerator/learn/how-refrigerators-work
- Compressor pressurizes the refrigerant gas (hot, high-pressure gas); condenser coils at the back or bottom release the heat to the room and the refrigerant turns to liquid: same sources; https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/book/export/html/2402
- Back/sides of a fridge feel warm because the condenser releases the heat taken from inside (manufacturer FAQs): https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/lg-refrigerator-why-does-the-exterior-of-my-refrigerator-feel-warm-CT10000021-20153132871539 ; https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Refrigeration/Full-Size_Refrigerators/Product_Info/Product_Assistance/Why_Does_the_Outside_of_the_Cabinet_Feel_Warm
- Capillary tube = thin restriction; pressure drops, lower pressure lowers the boiling point, refrigerant boils in the evaporator below the cabinet temperature and absorbs heat: https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/How_a_Refrigerator_Works ; https://www.achrnews.com/articles/114989-basic-thermodynamics-for-refrigeration-and-air-conditioning-part-2 ; https://swep.net/refrigerant-handbook/2.-compression-cycle/zx3
- Evaporator sits in the freezer; a fan pushes the cold air through the freezer and, through a damper (flap), into the fresh-food section: https://patents.google.com/patent/US5992166 ; https://patents.justia.com/patent/4009589 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US10001316
- A thermostat switches the compressor on and off: https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/How_a_Refrigerator_Works ; https://www.rtings.com/refrigerator/learn/how-refrigerators-work
- Frost forms on the evaporator; a defrost heater melts it periodically; meltwater drains to a pan under the fridge where compressor/condenser warmth evaporates it: https://academy.fredsappliance.com/refrigerator-training/how-refrigerators-work/ ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US4783971 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US6363736
- "Heat pump" framing (a fridge pumps heat out of the box): https://www.danfoss.com/en-us/service-and-support/fix-and-troubleshooting/cooling-installer-hub/the-fridge-how-it-works
Cut / not said: refrigerant names (R-134a vs R-600a varies by model), any temperatures, pressures or defrost intervals (sources vary: "every few hours" vs "~10 hours"), the condenser fan (not every fridge has one; the VO says "the warmth down there dries it up").
